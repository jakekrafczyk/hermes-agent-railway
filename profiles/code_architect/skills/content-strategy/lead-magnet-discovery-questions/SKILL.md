# Lead Magnet Discovery Questions

Build email-ready discovery question sets that extract domain expertise from a
stakeholder so you can build a high-value lead magnet (PDF, audit, checklist,
whitepaper, etc.) for their business.

## When to Use

The user is developing a content asset for a client / stakeholder and needs to
ask the right questions to scope it accurately. The user already has a strategy
document (GTM plan, brief, positioning doc) and needs questions the stakeholder
can answer by email.

## Process

### 1. Load the Strategy Document
Read the GTM plan, positioning brief, or strategy doc the user provides. Extract:
- Target audience and their pain points
- The specific lead magnet being developed (named in the doc)
- Value proposition and pitch angles
- Brand voice, trust signals, differentiation

### 2. Research the Domain
Search the web for industry best practices, common frameworks, benchmarks,
and competitor approaches relevant to the lead magnet topic. Extract 2–4
high-signal pages. Key things to surface:
- What does the industry consider "good" vs "bad" for this topic?
- What metrics or scoring rubrics exist?
- What red flags and hidden costs are commonly cited?
- What's the standard vetting / auditing process?

### 3. Bridge Research to Stakeholder Context
Synthesize questions that connect industry research with the stakeholder's
specific business. Each question should:
- Reference a specific insight from the GTM plan or research
- Ask the stakeholder to supply their real-world experience / data
- Have a clear purpose: the answer directly shapes the lead magnet's content

### 4. Format for Email
Output 4–6 questions in this format:

```
**N. Question text that is direct and scannable?**
1–3 sentences of context explaining why this matters for the asset,
referencing a specific detail from the GTM plan or research.
```

- Bold the question line, regular-weight the context below
- Questions should be numbered
- Entire block should be copy-paste ready
- No preamble, no closing — the user pastes directly into an email body

## Question Types That Work Well

| Type | Purpose | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **Scope boundary** | Nail down what the asset covers vs. excludes | "Should this cover carriers only, or carriers and brokers?" |
| **Depth / length** | Pin down format and commitment level | "How deep do you want this PDF to go — 10-minute scan or 30-minute diagnostic?" |
| **Real-world specifics** | Extract war stories, patterns, data | "What are the 2–3 biggest mistakes you see mid-market shippers make?" |
| **Red flags / pitfalls** | Surface the stakeholder's unique expertise | "What red flags do you personally look for that a shipper wouldn't think to check?" |
| **Tone / positioning** | Determine educational vs. sales balance | "How much should this set up your company as the obvious answer vs. being purely neutral?" |
| **CTA / next step** | Align the asset with the sales motion | "After they download this, what's the ideal next step — booking a call? A custom audit?" |

## Pitfalls

- **Don't ask things the GTM plan already answers.** If the doc says "mid-market manufacturers, $10M–$250M revenue," don't ask who the target audience is.
- **Don't mention format options already decided.** If the user told you it's a PDF, don't re-litigate quiz vs. PDF.
- **Keep context lines brief.** One to three sentences max. The stakeholder is busy.
- **Anchor every question in something concrete.** Avoid "what do you think about X?" — tie it to a specific detail from the plan or a number from the research.
